Evaluation Guidelines

🕵️ General Evaluation (The "BS" Filter)

  • Idea > Grammar: Ignore fancy words. Focus purely on whether their proposed solution actually makes sense for a student chapter.
  • The Constraint Test: Real human ideas mention limitations (time, budget, approvals, server limits). AI assumes unlimited resources and perfect execution.
  • SRMIST Context: Give extra points to candidates who mention specific campus logistics (venues, campus wifi issues, local student behavior).
  • The Buzzword Penalty: Penalize answers that rely on words like "synergy," "seamless," and "foster" but fail to explain the actual step-by-step process.

💻 Technical Domain

  • Pragmatism First: Do they suggest a realistic stack (e.g., React, Firebase, MySQL) or are they overcomplicating it with buzzwords (e.g., custom blockchain, AI models for basic tasks)?
  • Edge Case Awareness: The best candidates anticipate failure. Do they mention what happens if the database crashes, a form is submitted twice, or the UI breaks on mobile?
  • Implementation Speed: Does their solution sound like something a student could actually code in 1-2 weeks?

🎪 Events Domain

  • Chaos Management: Look for built-in time buffers (e.g., "I will keep a 15-minute gap for setup"). AI doesn't plan for human delays.
  • Crowd Logistics: Reject vague answers like "I will ensure a good atmosphere." Look for specifics: who is holding the mic, how are students routed to their seats, what is the backup venue?
  • Delegation: Strong event managers mention how they will use the volunteer team, rather than trying to do everything themselves.

🎨 Creatives Domain

  • Theme Adherence: (e.g., If the scenario involved your Greek God theme, did they naturally integrate the aesthetic, or just slap a label on a generic design?)
  • The Visualization Test: Can you clearly picture the UI, poster, or video they are describing in their answer? If it's too vague to visualize, it's a weak answer.
  • Platform Awareness: Do they consider constraints like Instagram grid layouts, aspect ratios, or mobile viewing?
